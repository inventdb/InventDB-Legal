00Rules before you build

Six things in this dataset will silently produce wrong reports if you don’t encode them. All six were found by testing the ledger, time and deadline tables against each other — not by reading the schema. Two of them are wrong in the shipped code.

1 · trust_ledger_cta.amount_in is empty on 1,829 deposit rows — now repaired

Summing the columns gave −$5,870,598 — not a balance, and not a number anyone should ever see against a client trust account. The cause was not the arithmetic. It was that amount_in was blank on 1,829 of the 2,217 deposit rows: every flat fee (unearned) (1,062), every retainer replenishment (755), and 12 settlement deposits.

The running balances always carried those deposits, so the missing figures were exactly recoverable as balance − previous_balance + amount_out. All 1,829 derived cleanly, recovering $7,139,500 — and the repaired total of $15,562,000 matches, to the cent, the money payments_and_receipts independently records as deposited to trust. Every one of the 1,829 has a matching payment row.

After the repair, amount_in − amount_out gives $1,268,901.63 — the running trust_account_balance, the sum of all 976 final client ledgers, and the figure the shipped tests already assert. The endpoints were right all along. Run backend/repair_dataset.py; if your instance still reports a negative trust balance, the repair has not been applied to it yet.

2 · Contingency time is not flagged at all — so WIP already excludes it

All 10,659 time entries on contingency matters have billable and billed null — not false, absent. The usual advice to filter contingency out of WIP is unnecessary here: any report keyed on billable = true and billed = false already excludes them by construction.

The consequence runs the other way. 2,931 of those entries are marked lodestar_eligible, and none of them will appear in any WIP or realisation report. Contingency effort has to be reported separately or it is invisible.

3 · Four obvious exception reports return nothing

Verified against the data, not assumed: 0 overdue deadlines (the earliest open due date is 2026-08-20, the day after the as-of date); 0 trust rows without a bank_cleared_date, so there are no deposits in transit or outstanding checks; 0 future in-court events without an appearing_name.

And calendared is null on all 804 open deadlines — only completed ones say Yes. An “uncalendared deadlines” report would flag the entire open docket every morning. Do not build it against this field.

4 · Two ageing columns, two different clocks

aging_bucket is computed from the due date; days_outstanding is computed from the issue date. On INV-2024-0014 they read 641 and 671 respectively, and both are right.

Both are frozen at 2026-08-19. Recomputing every bucket from the due date at that date reproduces the stored value on 470 of 470 outstanding invoices — which confirms the freeze exactly. For a chase list anyone will act on, derive from due_date against today.

5 · “Matters with no SOL deadline row” looks alarming and isn’t

238 open matters carry a limitations_date with no deadline row in the Statute of Limitations category. All 238 have already been filed. Filing is what satisfies the limitation, so the docket correctly stops tracking it.

Add and date_filed is empty and the list is zero. That filtered version is the report worth running; the unfiltered one is 238 rows of noise that will train people to ignore the alert.

6 · Three fields that looked like filters and weren’t — two repaired

costs.advanced_by_firm read Yes on all 8,027 rows, which made it useless. paid_from carries the real distinction, so the flag has been rewritten from it: 2,231 rows ($618,713) are genuinely advanced by the firm, 5,796 ($1,466,340) are not. Repaired.

intake.disposition only ever held Signed (137) or nothing (454) — yet 205 of the blanks carried a real decline_reason, and 386 rows used the literal string "0" where they meant no value. Both fixed: the book now reads 137 Signed / 205 Declined / 249 unresolved. Repaired.

court_calendar.continued_to is populated on 2,177 of 3,040 rows, but only 3 of those are future-dated — so filtering continuances out of a forward diary barely changes it. Not a defect, just far less important than it looks. Left as is.

✓  What did reconcile

Every headline count and total matches the shipped dashboard contract exactly — matters, clients, deadlines, time entries, unbilled hours (773.3), WIP value ($300,639.50) and AR outstanding ($778,108.56), 10 checks of 10.

Write-downs also tie across grains: $271,306 summed per time entry equals $271,306 summed per invoice, to the pound. That is unusual and worth relying on.

Only two things should ever interrupt a person

A negative client trust ledger (R‑07) and an MCLE lapse inside 60 days (R‑37). The first means the account cannot honestly answer an audit question; the second suspends a licence, and the remedy takes weeks.

Everything else waits for its slot. The moment a third alarm is added, all three begin to be ignored.

09What it can’t support

Each of these is a request a partner will make, and the honest answer. Saying so precisely here saves somebody a fortnight discovering it.

  • A firm profit and loss. There is no overhead ledger — no rent, salaries, insurance, software or marketing. costs_and_disbursements is client cost. What /api/reports/pnl returns is a revenue-and-client-cost summary; calling it a P&L in front of an accountant will not go well.
  • Profit per partner. Follows from the above. hourly_cost_rate supports contribution, which is real and useful, but it is not profit.
  • Time in each stage. matters.stage holds only the current value across 147 possible stages, with no history. Not derivable retrospectively.
  • Win rate. court_calendar.outcome describes what happened at a hearing, and appeared is only populated on 493 of 2,174 past events. No motion-level or case-level win rate exists.
  • A conflicts search. Conflict checking here is a date, a result and a clearer — not a searchable index of adverse parties and related entities. opposing_party gives you a text search, which is a start and not a system.
  • Utilisation by hour. time_entries.date has no time component. Daily granularity is the floor.
  • Any full-funnel intake history. Logging starts 5 Jan 2026 and 853 of 1,239 clients predate it. Conversion over the firm’s history compares a real numerator against a missing denominator.
  • Settlement benchmarks by carrier. 54 settlements across many carriers and matter types. Directional only, and never quoted without its count.
  • Deposits in transit or outstanding checks. bank_cleared_date is populated on all 5,755 trust rows, so the reconciling-items section of the three-way statement is structurally empty.
  • Anything from the documents. Files holds attachments with OCR and semantic search — genuinely good for finding a document. There is no structured extraction, so no report can derive a deadline, party or amount from a filed pleading.

Three defects left in place, deliberately

Five data defects were derivable and have been repaired (§00). These three are not derivable from the data, so repair_dataset.py reports them and changes nothing — inventing a value here would destroy the signal rather than restore it.

  • deadlines_and_sol.calendared — null on all 804 open deadlines. Every completed row says Yes, so the tempting fix is to stamp Yes on the rest. Don’t: “open and uncalendared” is a real and dangerous state, and asserting the opposite would erase the exact safety signal the field exists for. Capture it at source.
  • court_calendar.event_type — missing on 429 of 3,040 events, 61 of them future-dated. The hearing type cannot be inferred from the venue or the matter. The 61 future ones are the ones somebody has to fill in this week.
  • intake_and_leads.disposition — 249 leads with no outcome and no reason. This is a genuinely unresolved backlog, not a data-entry artefact. Work it; do not label it.

And one change that would retire most of §09

Write a row on every stage change. matters.stage holds only the current value across 147 possible stages, which is why cycle time, time-in-stage and any pipeline report are impossible. A single append-only history would make all of them exact within a quarter. It is the smallest change on this page with the largest payoff.
